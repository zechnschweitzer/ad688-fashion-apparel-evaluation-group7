Career Pathway and Industry Scope

Career Pathway and Industry Scope

Target career pathway: Business/Data Analyst roles

Industry code: NAICS 4481 (Clothing Stores), within the broader apparel and fashion retail sector

Industry label: Apparel and Fashion Retail

Research Rationale

Business and data analysts turn operational data into decisions organizations can act on. Apparel retail is a good place to study that work, since retailers are constantly reacting to seasonal demand, shifting consumer preferences, inventory limits, pricing calls, and supply chain pressure. Those conditions give analytical skills a direct, visible role in how the business runs.

For a student weighing an analytics career, it is not always obvious which skills employers actually care about, or whether their coursework and experience line up with what the job market expects. A title like “business analyst” or “data analyst” hides a lot of variation: some mix of technical ability, analytical thinking, communication, and business judgment, and the exact mix shifts by employer. This project tries to pin down which skills matter most for business/data analyst roles in apparel retail specifically, then checks that list against our own team’s current skills.

That is what separates this from a plain labor-market summary: it is meant to work as a career evaluation tool. A student should be able to look at it and see what employers are asking for, where their own strengths already line up, and what still needs work before they apply.

Literature Review

Umamaheswaran et al. (2023) looked at what employers ask for in business analytics postings and found a consistent mix: big data, business intelligence, and analytical technique on one side, communication and stakeholder management on the other. That matters for how we think about readiness. Knowing the software is not enough on its own; an analyst also has to understand the business problem and explain results to people who are not going to read the code.

Apparel retail is a fitting test case for that combination. Forecasting, inventory, pricing, and supply chain decisions all show up as recurring analytical needs in clothing retail, and IBISWorld (2025) backs that up with broader industry context on clothing and clothing accessory retailers. Demand in this sector moves fast and shifts with the seasons, which is part of why data-supported decisions carry more weight here than they might in a slower-moving industry.

Retail hiring overall backs up the case for treating analytics as its own career path within the industry. The Planet Group (2025) found that retail hiring has gotten more selective even as it leans harder into technology, analytics, and customer experience. So while fewer people may be getting hired overall, the analytical roles that support pricing, inventory, operations, and customer decisions do not seem to be losing ground.

Job postings themselves give a more concrete look at what these roles ask for. Scanning ZipRecruiter listings, SQL, Excel, and a visualization tool like Tableau or Power BI show up repeatedly, alongside the usual asks for analytical and communication skills (ZipRecruiter, 2026a). We are treating this as a starting point rather than a finding, since individual job postings vary a lot by employer, seniority, and timing.

The same postings also show a fair amount of spread in pay and remote availability (ZipRecruiter, 2026b). Salary and remote work are not the central question we are asking, but they are useful context for judging how attractive a given role is. Once we are working with the MET Employability Career Match data, we will be able to check whether these early patterns hold up within our apparel retail scope specifically.

Across these sources, the same picture keeps showing up: business/data analyst roles need technical skill, analytical thinking, and the ability to work with the business side, all at once. What none of this tells us yet is which of those skills matter most specifically in apparel retail, or how close our own skills already are to that bar. That gap is what this project is built to close.

Target User

This product is aimed at BU MET students who are exploring analytics career paths and want a clearer picture of which industries and skills to prioritize. Our goal is to help someone in this position understand what a business/data analyst role in apparel retail actually looks like, so they can decide whether it fits their interests and know what to learn next if it does.

Product Question

Which skills are most valued for business/data analyst roles in apparel retail and how well does our team’s current skill set match those demands?

MET Employability Career Match Fields We Expect to Use

We plan to use title/normalized_title, naics_code, skills, annual_salary_min/annual_salary_max, remote_status, city/state, and soc_code. These fields should let us see which skills and pay levels matter most for business/data analyst roles in apparel retail.
